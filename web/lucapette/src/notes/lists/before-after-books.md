I often bring up this concept I call "before/after". The idea is
pretty simple: there are some books you can distinctly tell marked a change in
your life. There's a before you read that and an after you read it. It also
applies to music and cinema.

So here's the list in no particular order (I'm doing this for a friend right 
now so it may change):

## Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat

{% book "50c26112-477d-44d6-a771-10e4f98f0d1e", "Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat: Mastering the Elements of Good Cooking", "before-after-books/salt-fat-acid-heat.jpg", "200", "https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/50c26112-477d-44d6-a771-10e4f98f0d1e" %}

I've been cooking since I'm 13 years old and this book definitely changed my 
approach to cooking. Before reading it, I wasn't as systematic in my 
seasoning and definitely had a poor understanding of heat. After reading it, 
I became a better home cook and started to venture myself further from the 
cooking I had been doing.

{% endbook %}

## The Three-Body Problem

{% book "bd4012e7-ab1c-4cdf-9561-6c8dc6bc6218", "The Three-Body Problem", "before-after-books/three-body-problem.jpg", "200", "https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/bd4012e7-ab1c-4cdf-9561-6c8dc6bc6218" %}

Cixin Liu is one of my absolute favourite science-fiction writers. 
His imagination is immense and I love his ability to play and mix the tiniest 
with the biggest things in the universe. The reason "Remembrance of Earth's 
Past" (yep that's the name of the three book series) is part of this list is 
quite simple. Up to this book, I had never come across any alien life form 
that was truly alien to me. Every book or movie I had watched on first 
contact had always felt like a projection of hopes and fear of a human. This 
series is different though. I have this distinct memory of missing my tram 
station while reading the first pages of the second book because I was too 
shocked to get up from my seat. Been recommending this to everyone ever since.

{% endbook %}

## A Fortune-Teller Told Me

{% book "895cbf09-466d-4f4d-885c-8a1299a87d75", "A Fortune-Teller Told Me: Earthbound Travels in the Far East", "before-after-books/a-fortune-teller-told-me.jpg", "200", "https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/895cbf09-466d-4f4d-885c-8a1299a87d75" %}

Before I read a fortune-teller told me I had a decisively more analytical 
approach to life choices. This book helped me to see through the noise of 
life and accept that most choices are in fact not rational and that maybe 
that's the best part of it. I've gone back to this book every decade which 
is something I do very rarely.

{% endbook %}


## Parable of the Sower

{% book "a5413fa0-71d9-4881-8d39-46ba01578134", "Parable of the Sower", "before-after-books/parable-of-the-sower.jpg", "200", "https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/a5413fa0-71d9-4881-8d39-46ba01578134" %}

Octavia Butler is, by far, the most incredible science-fiction writer I read. 
Emotionally, going through her books is an impossibly painful yet rewarding 
journey.

The parable series is really impossible to describe in words. You have to 
read it yourself to understand how deeply prescient Butler is and how crude 
and real and deep is her understanding of American (and, at large, 
capitalist) society.

{% endbook %}

## Recognizing the Stranger

{% book "92760d25-c13b-47a4-905b-e0d84dbe907b", "Recognizing the Stranger: On Palestine and Narrative", "before-after-books/recognizing-the-stranger.jpg", "200", "https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/92760d25-c13b-47a4-905b-e0d84dbe907b" %}

"To remain human at this juncture is to remain in agony. Let us remain there: it is the more honest place from which to speak."

This quote helped me navigate post Oct. 7 western discourse (which is in 
itself a before/after of gigantic proportions). Before reading this very 
short book I had trouble accepting my own state of mind in relation to the 
average westard out there. After it though, I know my truth. 

{% endbook %}


## Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone

{% book "43aadc02-43e9-4512-88eb-c72288ead79e", "Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone", "before-after-books/harry-potter-and-the-sorcerers-stone.jpg", "200", "https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/43aadc02-43e9-4512-88eb-c72288ead79e" %}

All right this one is odd as the series, while well written and entertaining,
is nowhere close to the quality of any other book in this list. Also I'd 
rather not "promote" the author. But she obviously doesn't need that anyhow 
and I want to stay thoughtful to my own definition. Before reading this 
series, I could hardly converse in English. After reading it (while also 
listening to the audiobooks), I was totally fluent. Definitely fits the 
definition!

{% endbook %}

## The Inmates Are Running the Asylum

{% book "af0f67a2-7629-4c65-a87c-4f44cae3f8e8", "The Inmates Are Running the Asylum: Why High Tech Products Drive Us Crazy and How to Restore the Sanity", "before-after-books/the-inmates-are-running-the-asylum.jpg", "200", "https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/af0f67a2-7629-4c65-a87c-4f44cae3f8e8" %}

This is the only book related to my profession on this list now that I think 
about it. That's interesting in itself in a way. But the main point here is 
that before reading this book I had very little understanding of the product 
side of the value chain of sofware development. After I read this book, I 
became much more aware of how to work with product people, how to manage 
them, how to help them. Seminal reading for my professional development. 

{% endbook %}
